msgid "This is the opening chapter of the book. The author says: Having once passed through a dream-like experience, he concealed the true events and, borrowing the notion of \"spiritual intelligence,\" wrote this book, The Story of the Stone. Hence the name \"Zhen Shiyin\" and so forth. But what events and what people does the book record? He further says: \"Now, busy and bustling in the dust of the world, I have accomplished nothing. Suddenly I thought of all the women of former days, and examining them one by one, I felt that their conduct and insight were all superior to mine. How is it that I, a dignified man, am truly inferior to those women? Indeed, shame is more than enough, regret is useless, and it is a day of utter helplessness! At this time, I wished to take the favors of Heaven's grace and ancestral virtue I once relied upon, the days of fine clothes and rich food, the kindness of my father and elder brother in education, the virtue of teachers and friends in admonition, and the crime of having accomplished no skill to this day, half a lifetime wasted—compile them into a collection to tell the world: my guilt cannot be avoided, but among the women of the inner chambers there were indeed those who stood out one by one. I must not, because of my own unworthiness and self-protective concealment of faults, let them all be obliterated. Although today my dwelling is of thatched rafters and reed windows, with earthen stove and rope bed, the morning breeze and evening dew, the willows by the steps and flowers in the courtyard, do not hinder my bosom and my brush. Though I am unlearned and my writing lacks literary grace, what harm is there in using rustic speech and village talk to spin out a tale, so that the women of the inner chambers may be transmitted to posterity, and also delight the eyes of the world and break people's melancholy? Is that not fitting?\" Hence the name \"Jia Yucun\" and so forth."
