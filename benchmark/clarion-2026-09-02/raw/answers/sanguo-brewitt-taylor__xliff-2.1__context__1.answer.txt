          <target>In the fourth month of the second year of Chien-ning, on the day of full moon, the Emperor ascended the throne in the Hall of Warm Virtue. As he took his seat, a fierce wind suddenly arose in the corner of the hall, and a great blue serpent was seen gliding down from the beam, coiling itself upon the chair. The Emperor fell back in fright, and his attendants hurriedly carried him into the palace, while all the officials fled in confusion. In a moment the serpent vanished. Then came a sudden clap of thunder and a downpour of rain, with hail, which lasted until midnight and destroyed countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Chien-ning, there was an earthquake in Lo-yang; and the sea overflowed its bounds, so that the dwellers along the coast were all swept away by the great waves. In the first year of Kuang-ho, hens were changed into cocks. On the first day of the sixth month, a black vapour, more than ten chang in length, flew into the Hall of Warm Virtue. In the seventh month of autumn, a rainbow appeared in the Jade Hall; and the cliffs of Wu-yuan all crumbled and split asunder. These omens of ill were manifold, not of one kind alone.</target>

          <target>The Emperor issued a decree asking his ministers the cause of these calamities. The Gentleman-Consultant Ts'ai Yung submitted a memorial, saying that the falling of the rainbow and the change of hens into cocks were due to the interference of women and eunuchs in government, and his words were blunt and outspoken. The Emperor sighed as he read the memorial, and then rose to change his robes. Ts'ao Chieh, who was behind him, secretly observed this and told all his associates. Thereupon they falsely accused Ts'ai Yung of other crimes and sent him back to his native village. Later, Chang Jang, Chao Chung, Feng Hsu, Tuan Kuei, Ts'ao Chieh, Hou Lan, Chien Shuo, Ch'eng K'uang, Hsia Yun, and Kuo Sheng, ten in all, leagued together in villainy and were known as the "Ten Regular Attendants." The Emperor honoured and trusted Chang Jang, calling him "Foster Father," and the government daily grew worse, until the hearts of all the people turned to thoughts of rebellion and robbers rose like swarms of bees.</target>
