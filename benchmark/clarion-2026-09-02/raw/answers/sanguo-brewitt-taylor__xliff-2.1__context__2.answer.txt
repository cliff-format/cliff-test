          <target>On the fifteenth day of the fourth moon, in the second year of Chien-ning, the Emperor ascended the throne in the Warm Virtue Hall. Scarcely had he taken his seat when a furious wind arose in the corner of the hall, and from the beams above a great green serpent descended, coiling itself upon the chair. The Emperor fell back in terror, and his attendants hurried him into the palace, while the assembled officials scattered in flight. In a moment the serpent vanished. Then came a sudden crash of thunder and a deluge of rain, with hail that fell until midnight, destroying countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Chien-ning, an earthquake shook Loyang; and the sea rose in flood, sweeping the dwellers along the coast into the waves. In the first year of Kuang-ho, hens were transformed into cocks. On the first day of the sixth moon, a black vapour more than ten chang in length flew into the Warm Virtue Hall. In the seventh moon of autumn, a rainbow appeared in the Jade Hall; and the cliffs of Wuyuan crumbled and split apart. Omens of ill fortune such as these were not few.</target>

          <target>The Emperor issued a decree asking his ministers the cause of these calamities. The Gentleman-Consultant Ts'ai Yung submitted a memorial, saying that the falling rainbows and the changing hens were due to the interference of women and eunuchs in government, and his words were blunt and outspoken. The Emperor sighed as he read the memorial, and then rose to change his robes. Ts'ao Chieh, watching secretly from behind, reported everything to those about him. Thereupon he framed other charges against Ts'ai Yung and had him dismissed to his native village. Later, Chang Jang, Chao Chung, Feng Hsu, Tuan Kuei, Ts'ao Chieh, Hou Lan, Chien Shuo, Ch'eng K'uang, Hsia Yun, and Kuo Sheng, ten in all, leagued together in villainy and were known as the "Ten Regular Attendants." The Emperor honored and trusted Chang Jang, calling him "Foster Father," and the government daily grew worse, until the hearts of the people turned to disorder and robbers rose like swarms of bees.</target>
