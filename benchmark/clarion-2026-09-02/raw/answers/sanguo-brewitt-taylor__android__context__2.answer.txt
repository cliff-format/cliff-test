    <string name="chapter-1-001">On the fifteenth day of the fourth month in the second year of Jianning, the Emperor held court in the Hall of Warm Virtue. As he had just taken his seat, a violent wind suddenly rose in the corner of the hall, and a great green serpent was seen flying down from the beams, coiling itself upon the chair. The Emperor fell back in fright, and his attendants hurriedly carried him into the palace, while all the officials fled in confusion. In a moment, the serpent vanished. Then came a sudden clap of thunder and a heavy rain, accompanied by hail, which did not cease until midnight, destroying countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Jianning, there was an earthquake in Luoyang; and again the sea overflowed its bounds, and the dwellers along the coast were all swept into the sea by the great waves. In the first year of Guanghe, hens turned into roosters. On the first day of the sixth month, a black vapor more than ten zhang long flew into the Hall of Warm Virtue. In the seventh month of autumn, a rainbow appeared in the Jade Hall; and the mountains and banks of Wuyuan all collapsed and split apart. These various omens of misfortune were not limited to a single instance.</string>

    <string name="chapter-1-002">The Emperor issued an edict asking the assembled ministers the cause of these calamities and portents. The Gentleman Consultant Cai Yong submitted a memorial, holding that the falling of the rainbow and the changing of hens were due to the interference of women and eunuchs in government, and his words were quite blunt and direct. The Emperor, reading the memorial, sighed, and then rose to change his clothes. Cao Jie, who was behind him, secretly observed this and told all those around him. Subsequently, he framed Cai Yong on other charges and had him banished to his native village. Later, Zhang Rang, Zhao Zhong, Feng Xu, Duan Gui, Cao Jie, Hou Lan, Jian Shuo, Cheng Kuang, Xia Yun, and Guo Sheng—ten men—colluded in villainy and were known as the "Ten Regular Attendants."
