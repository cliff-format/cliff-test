          <target>On the fifteenth day of the fourth moon, in the second year of Chien-ning, the Emperor ascended the throne in the Warm Virtue Hall. Scarcely had he taken his seat when a fierce whirlwind arose in the corner of the hall, and from the beams above a great blue serpent descended, coiling itself upon the throne. The Emperor, struck with terror, fell backward, and his attendants hurried him into the palace, while the assembled officials fled in confusion. In a moment the serpent vanished. Then suddenly came a tremendous clap of thunder and a violent rain, with hail, which lasted until midnight and destroyed countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Chien-ning, an earthquake shook Loyang; and the sea, overflowing its bounds, swept the dwellers along the coast into its depths. In the first year of Kuang-ho, hens were transformed into cocks. On the first day of the sixth moon, a black vapor, more than ten chang in length, flew into the Warm Virtue Hall. In the seventh moon of autumn, a rainbow appeared in the Jade Hall; and the cliffs of Wuyuan all crumbled and split asunder. These omens of ill, and many more, were not of one kind alone.</target>

          <target>The Emperor issued an edict asking his ministers the cause of these calamities. The Gentleman-Consultant Ts'ai Yung submitted a memorial, saying that the falling rainbows and the transformation of hens were due to the interference of women and eunuchs in government. His words were blunt and direct. The Emperor, reading the memorial, sighed, and rose to change his robes. Ts'ao Chieh, watching from behind, reported all to his associates. Thereupon they falsely accused Ts'ai Yung of other crimes and sent him back to his native fields. Later, Chang Jang, Chao Chung, Feng Hsu, Tuan Kuei, Ts'ao Chieh, Hou Lan, Chien Shuo, Ch'eng K'uang, Hsia Yun, and Kuo Sheng, ten in all, leagued together in villainy and were known as the "Ten Regular Attendants." The Emperor honored and trusted Chang Jang, calling him "Foster Father," and the government daily grew worse, until the hearts of all the people turned to thoughts of rebellion and robbers rose like swarms of bees.</target>
