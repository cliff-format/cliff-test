          <target>In the second year of Chien Ning, on the fifteenth day of the fourth month, the Emperor went into the Hall Wen-te. As he was ascending the throne, a violent wind suddenly arose from the corner of the Hall, and there was seen a huge green serpent flying down from the beam and coiling upon the throne. The Emperor was terrified and fell; his attendants hurriedly assisted him into the palace, and all the officials fled and hid themselves. In a moment the serpent disappeared. Suddenly came a great thunderstorm with hail, which fell until midnight and did not cease, destroying countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Chien Ning, there was an earthquake in Loyang; again the sea overflowed, and the inhabitants along the coast were all swept into the sea by the great waves. In the first year of Kuang Ho, a hen was changed into a cock. On the first day of the sixth month, a black vapor more than ten fathoms long flew into the Hall Wen-te. In the seventh month of autumn, a rainbow appeared in the Jade Hall; the cliffs of Wu Yuan all collapsed. These various portents were not confined to one kind.</target>

          <target>The Emperor issued an edict asking the assembled ministers the cause of these calamities. The Privy Counselor Ts'ai Yung presented a memorial, saying that the falling of the rainbow and the changing of the hen were due to the interference of women and eunuchs in government, and his words were quite blunt and direct. The Emperor, having read the memorial, sighed, and rose to change his garments. Ts'ao Chieh, who was behind, secretly watched him, and told everything to the attendants. Then he involved Ts'ai Yung in crime on some other pretext, and had him sent back to his fields. Afterwards, Chang Jang, Chao Chung, Feng Hsü, Tuan Kuei, Ts'ao Chieh, Hou Lan, Chien Shuo, Ch'eng K'uang, Hsia Yün, and Kuo Sheng, ten men, banded together for wickedness, and were known as the "Ten Eunuchs." The Emperor honored and trusted Chang Jang, calling him "Father," and the government daily grew worse, so that the hearts of the people were inclined to disorder, and robbers and rebels sprang up like swarms of bees.</target>

He was seven feet five inches tall, his ears hung down to his shoulders, and his hands reached below his knees; his eyes could turn and see his own ears; his face was like a crown of jade, and his lips as if painted with vermilion.
