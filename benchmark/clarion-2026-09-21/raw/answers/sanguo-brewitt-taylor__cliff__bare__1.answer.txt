The Emperor also favoured the Lady Wang, who bore the prince Xie. The Empress He, in her jealousy, poisoned the Lady Wang. The prince Xie was brought up in the palace of the Empress Dowager Dong. This Empress Dowager Dong was the mother of the Emperor Ling and the wife of Liu Chang, Marquis of Xiedu Village. In former days, because the Emperor Huan had no son, Liu Chang's son had been brought to the throne, and this was the Emperor Ling. <chapter-2-004>
source: "董太后大喜。次日設朝，董太后降旨，封皇子協為陳留王，董重為驃騎將軍，張讓 等共預朝政。何太后見董太后專權，於宮中設一宴，請董太后赴席。酒至半酣，何太后 起身捧盃再拜曰：「我等皆婦人也，參預朝政，非其所宜。昔呂后因握重權，宗族千口 皆被戮。今我等宜深居九重；朝廷大事，任大臣元老自行商議，此國家之幸也。願垂聽 焉。」董太后大怒曰：「汝鴆死王美人，設心嫉妒。今倚汝子為君，與汝兄何進之勢， 輒敢亂言！吾敕驃騎斷汝兄首，如反掌耳！」何后亦怒曰：「吾以好言相勸，何反怒耶 ？」董后曰：「汝家屠沽小輩，有何見識！」"
target: "The Empress Dowager Dong was greatly pleased. The next day, when court was held, she issued her decree, creating the prince Xie Prince of Chenliu, making Dong Zhong General of the Flying Cavalry, and giving Zhang Rang and his fellows a share in the government. The Empress Dowager He, seeing the Empress Dowager Dong monopolize the power, gave a banquet in the palace and invited her to it. When the wine had gone halfway, the Empress Dowager He rose, holding her cup, and bowed twice, saying: 'We are both women, and it is not fitting that we should take part in the affairs of state. Long ago, because the Empress Lü grasped great power, a thousand mouths of her clan were put to the sword. Now we should dwell retired within the nine-fold palace and leave the great affairs of the court to the ministers and elders to discuss among themselves; that would be the good fortune of the state. I pray you to hear me.' The Empress Dowager Dong flew into a rage, saying: 'You poisoned the Lady Wang to death out of the jealousy in your heart. Now, leaning upon your son the Emperor and upon the power of your brother He Jin, you dare to talk recklessly! If I bid the Flying Cavalry General cut off your brother's head, it is as easy as turning my hand.' The Empress He too was angry and said: 'I gave you good counsel; why are you angry instead?' The Empress Dowager Dong said: 'You are a family of butchers and wine-sellers; what do you know of anything?'"

target: "In a little while He Jin went in and reported to the Empress Dowager, wishing to put the eunuchs to death. The Empress Dowager He said: 'It has ever been the custom of the Han that the eunuchs should command the palace. The late Emperor has but newly left the world, and you would slay his old servants; that is no way to honour the ancestral temple.' He Jin was by nature a man of no decision; hearing the Empress Dowager's words, he answered submissively and went out. Yuan Shao came to meet him and asked: 'How goes the great matter?' He Jin said: 'The Empress Dowager will not consent; what is to be done?' Shao said: 'You may summon the bold men and heroes from every quarter to bring their troops to the capital and put all these eunuchs to the sword. The matter is urgent, and the Empress Dowager will not be able to refuse.' He Jin said: 'That is a most excellent plan!' And he sent out proclamations to every garrison, calling them to the capital."

target: "Chen Lin, the Master of the Records, said: 'That will not do. As the saying goes, to shut the eyes and catch a sparrow is to cheat oneself. Even a small creature cannot be taken by deceit, and how much less the great affairs of a state? Now, general, you lean upon the imperial majesty and hold the keys of the army; you stride like a dragon and a tiger, and you can set high and low at your will. If you wish to slay the eunuchs, it is no more than blowing up a great furnace to burn a hair. You have only to act at once and decide with authority, and Heaven and man will go with you. But instead you send proclamations abroad to the great ministers and bring them to the very gates of the capital, where heroes will gather, each with a mind of his own — that is what is called holding the spear reversed and giving the handle into another's hand. Even if the deed were done, disorder would arise from it.' He Jin laughed and said: 'That is the view of a coward!' A man beside him clapped his hands and laughed aloud, saying: 'This is as easy as turning one's hand; why debate it so?' They looked at him, and it was Cao Cao. Truly: Would you rid the ruler's side of knavish turmoil, you must heed the wise men's counsel at court. What words Cao Cao spoke, we shall hear in the next chapter."
