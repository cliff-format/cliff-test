    <group id="paper.abstract">
      <unit id="paper-title">
        <segment state="translated">
          <source>Auditing Consent Records at Scale: A Field Study of Three Consent Portals</source>
          <target>大规模审计同意记录：三个同意门户的实地研究</target>
        </segment>
      </unit>
      <unit id="paper-abstract-opening">
        <segment state="translated">
          <source>Consent records are the evidence a company relies on when a regulator asks why a person's data was used, yet the records themselves are rarely audited. We report a field study of three consent portals run by mid-sized companies over nine months.</source>
          <target>同意记录是公司在监管机构询问为何使用某人的数据时所依赖的证据，然而这些记录本身却很少受到审计。我们报告了一项对三家由中型公司运营的同意门户为期九个月的实地研究。</target>
        </segment>
      </unit>
      <unit id="paper-abstract-method">
        <segment state="translated">
          <source>Each portal was exercised with a fixed script of routine requests, and every receipt it returned was compared against the entry that the portal had written to its own log.</source>
          <target>每个门户都使用固定的常规请求脚本进行测试，并将其返回的每一份回执与门户写入自身日志的条目进行比对。</target>
        </segment>
      </unit>
      <unit id="paper-abstract-findings">
        <segment state="translated">
          <source>Two of the three portals lost the timestamp of at least one withdrawal, and none of them could show, without manual work, which version of the notice a person had agreed to. We argue that a consent record is worth no more than the audit trail behind it, and we publish the test script so that other teams can repeat the study.</source>
          <target>三个门户中有两个丢失了至少一次撤回的时间戳，而且如果没有人工操作，它们都无法显示某人同意的通知版本。我们认为，同意记录的价值不超过其背后的审计追踪，并且我们发布测试脚本，以便其他团队可以重复该研究。</target>
        </segment>
      </unit>
