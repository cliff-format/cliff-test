msgstr "After a while He Jin entered and reported to the Empress Dowager that he wished to execute the palace eunuchs. Empress Dowager He said: 'It is the old practice of the Han for eunuchs to command the forbidden palace. The late Emperor has just left the world, and you wish to kill his old servants; that is not to respect the ancestral temple.' He Jin was by nature a man of no decision; hearing the Empress Dowager's words, he assented and withdrew. Yuan Shao met him and asked: 'How stands the great matter?' He Jin said: 'The Empress Dowager will not consent; what can be done?' Yuan Shao said: 'You may summon heroes and men of valor from all quarters to lead troops to the capital and put all the eunuchs to death. At this urgent moment, the Empress Dowager will have no choice but to comply.' He Jin said: 'This plan is excellent!' He at once sent proclamations to the various garrisons, summoning them to the capital."

msgstr "The Chief Clerk Chen Lin said: 'That will not do! As the saying goes, "Cover your eyes and catch sparrows"—that is self-deception. Even small creatures cannot be caught by deceiving oneself, let alone great affairs of state. Now, General, you rely on imperial authority and hold the key to military power; you stride like a dragon and a tiger, and high and low are in your heart. If you wish to execute the eunuchs, it is like blowing on a great furnace to singe a hair. You need only act quickly, exercise authority, and decide at once; then Heaven and man will obey you. But instead you would send proclamations to outside ministers, causing them to come to the capital gates. Heroes will gather, each with a different mind. This is what is called holding the shield and spear upside down and giving the handle to another; the enterprise will surely fail and disorder will arise instead.' He Jin laughed and said: 'That is the view of a coward!' A man beside him clapped his hands and laughed loudly, saying: 'This matter is as easy as turning one's hand; why discuss it so much?' They looked at him—it was Cao Cao. Thus it is said: If you would remove the mischief of petty men around the ruler, you must heed the counsel of the wise in court.
