At the end of every workday Maren walked the harbour road the way a clock walks its dial, and each evening the last thing she heard before the dark came down was the tin wind-bell over the chandler's door, telling the street that the tide had turned and that the world, for a little while, would go on without her. She did not hurry. The salt worked its way into everything she owned, and she had stopped minding it years ago.</source>

          <source>I never knew whether the bell was a comfort or a reproach. It rang in the same key as my mother's name, the way the tide writes the same line on the sand and then rubs it out. I wanted to keep the sound, the way a child keeps a stone, but a sound has no pocket to be kept in, and so I keep it in my chest and pretend it does not weigh anything.</source>
          <target state="translated">我从来不知道那铃是安慰，还是责备。它响起来的调子，和我母亲的名字是同一个调；就像潮水在沙上写下同一条线，又把它抹掉。我想留住那声音，像孩子留着一块石头那样；可声音没有口袋可装，于是我把它收在胸口，假装它没有重量。</target>
        </segment>
      </unit>
      <unit id="monologue-stone">
        <segment>
          <source>The stone I kept as a girl is still under the floorboard where I left it, and if the house burned I would not grieve for the stone. But the bell is not kept anywhere; it is only heard, and a heard thing has to be heard again or it was never there at all. I keep going back, then, not to keep the bell, but to keep the hearing of it.</source>
          <target state="translated">我小时候留着的那块石头，还在我放它的那块地板下面；就算房子烧了，我也不会为那块石头难过。可那铃并不存放在任何地方；它只是被听见，而被听见的东西必须一再被听见，否则它根本不曾存在。于是我一次次回去，不是为了留住那铃，而是为了留住听见它的那一刻。</target>

Rain hung offshore in a grey curtain that never quite reached the quay.
