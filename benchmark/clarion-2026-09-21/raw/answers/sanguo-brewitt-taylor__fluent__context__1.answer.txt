chapter-1-001 = In the second year of Jian Ning, on the fifteenth day of the fourth month, the Emperor held court in the Hall of Warm Virtue. He had scarce ascended the throne when a wild wind sprang up from a corner of the hall, and there came a great green serpent flying down from the beam, which coiled itself upon the seat. The Emperor fell down in terror; his attendants hurried him into the palace, and every officer fled for his life. In a moment the serpent was gone. Then of a sudden came a mighty storm of thunder and rain, with hail that did not cease until midnight and wrecked countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Jian Ning, an earthquake shook Luoyang; the sea again overflowed its bounds, and the people along the coast were swept by the great waves into the deep. In the first year of Guang He, hens were turned into cocks. On the first day of the sixth month, a black vapor more than ten cubits long flew into the Hall of Warm Virtue. In the seventh month of autumn a rainbow was seen above the Jade Hall; and the cliffs of Wuyuan crumbled and fell on every side. Such evil omens were not one alone.

Cai Yong, a Gentleman of the Court, submitted a memorial, holding that the falling of the rainbow and the change of the hens came of women and eunuchs meddling in the government; his words were blunt and outspoken. As the Emperor read the memorial he sighed, and rose to change his robes. Cao Jie, watching him secretly from behind, made all of it known to those about him. Afterwards Zhang Rang, Zhao Zhong, Feng Xu, Duan Gui, Cao Jie, Hou Lan, Jian Shuo, Cheng Kuang, Xia Yun, and Guo Sheng, ten in all, banded together in villainy and were known as the Ten Eunuchs. chapter-1-006 = This man cared little for books. Of a mild and generous temper, sparing of speech, he let neither joy nor anger show on his face; but his ambitions were great, and above all he loved to make friends of the valiant men of the empire. He stood seven feet five inches tall; his ears were long and hung to his shoulders, and his hands reached below his knees. He could turn his eyes and see his own ears; his face was as fair as a crown of jade, and his lips as though painted with vermilion. He was of the line of Liu Sheng, Prince Jing of Zhongshan, and a great-great-grandson of the Emperor Jing of Han. His surname was Liu, his name Bei, his style Xuande. In former days Liu Zhen, son of Liu Sheng, had been made Marquis of Zhuolu in the reign of Emperor Wu; afterwards he lost his rank for failing in the gold tribute, and so this branch of the family was left stranded in Zhuo. Xuande's grandfather was Liu Xiong and his father Liu Hong. Liu Hong had been recommended as Filial and Incorrupt and had also served as an official, but he died early. Xuande, left an orphan as a child, served his mother with the utmost devotion. The family was poor, and he made a living by selling straw sandals and weaving mats. They lived in the village of Lou Sang in that county. To the south-east of their house stood a great mulberry tree, more than fifty feet high, which from afar looked like the canopy of a carriage. A physiognomist once said, "This house is sure to produce a man of high estate."

We ask not to be born in the same year, the same month, and the same day, but only that we may die in the same year, the same month, and the same day. Heaven above and Earth below, behold this heart of ours. If we betray our bond and forget your grace, may Heaven and man alike put us to death." When the oath was done, Xuande was revered as the elder brother, Guan Yu next, and Zhang Fei the youngest. The sacrifice to Heaven and Earth finished, they slaughtered the ox and set out wine, and gathered the brave men of the village, more than three hundred of them, and there in the peach garden drank deep until they were merry. On the morrow they put their weapons in order, and their only regret was that they had no horses to ride.

chapter-2-004 = The Empress Dowager Dong was overjoyed. Next day, when court was held, she sent down her decree, creating the prince Liu Xie as Prince of Chenliu, making Dong Zhong General of the Flying Cavalry, and admitting Zhang Rang and his fellows to a share in the government. When the Empress Dowager He saw the Empress Dowager Dong grasping at sole power, she prepared a banquet in the palace and invited her to be a guest at it. When the wine had gone round and they were half merry, the Empress Dowager He rose, lifted her cup, and bowed twice, saying, "We are all of us women, and it is not seemly for us to meddle in the government. In former days, because the Empress Lü held the power in her hands, a thousand of her kin were put to the sword. Now we should keep retired within the inner palace; let the great affairs of state be left to the ministers and the elders of the court to settle among themselves — that would be the good fortune of the realm. I pray you to hear me." The Empress Dowager Dong flew into a passion: "You poisoned Lady Wang to death, for your heart is eaten up with jealousy. And now, leaning on the fact that your son is Emperor and on the power of your brother He Jin, you dare to talk such nonsense! I have but to order the General of the Flying Cavalry to strike off your brother's head — as easy as turning over my hand!" The Empress He too grew angry: "I gave you kind counsel — why do you fly into a rage?" The Empress Dowager Dong retorted, "You come of a family of butchers and wine-sellers; what understanding can you have?"

chapter-2-005 = Presently He Jin went in and laid before the Empress his wish to put the eunuchs to death. The Empress He said, "It is an old usage of the Han house that the palace attendants command the inner palaces. The late Emperor has but newly departed this world, and you would slay his old servants — that is no way to show respect for the ancestral temple." He Jin was by nature a man of no decision; hearing the Empress Dowager's words, he went out murmuring assent. Yuan Shao met him and asked, "How goes the great matter?" He Jin said, "The Empress Dowager will not permit it — what is to be done?" Shao replied, "You may summon the heroes and men of note from all quarters to bring their troops to the capital and put every one of these eunuchs to death. At such a pass as this, she cannot refuse." He Jin cried, "A most excellent plan!" — and at once sent proclamations to every garrison, calling them to the capital.

chapter-2-006 = Chen Lin, the Master of the Records, said, "You must not do it! The proverb says, 'Shut your eyes and snare the sparrows,' and that is to cheat yourself. You cannot even deceive the little birds and succeed — how much less in the great affairs of a state! Now, General, you lean upon the imperial majesty and hold the military power in your hands; you step like a dragon and a tiger, and the rise and fall of all things is in your heart. If you would put the eunuchs to death, it is no more than blowing up a great furnace to singe a hair. You need only act quickly, wield your authority and decide on the spot — then Heaven and man will follow you. But instead you send proclamations out to the great officers and summon them to the very gates of the capital: heroes gathered together, each with a purpose of his own. That is what is called holding the spear reversed and handing the handle to another. The deed may be done, but disorder will spring from it." He Jin laughed and said, "That is the view of a coward!" At his side a man clapped his hands and laughed aloud: "This matter is as easy as turning over one's hand — why all this debate?" They looked at him: it was Cao Cao. And so it is said: Would you sweep the knaves from about the throne and end the strife, you must heed the counsel of the wise men of the court. What words Cao Cao spoke, you shall hear in the chapter that follows.
