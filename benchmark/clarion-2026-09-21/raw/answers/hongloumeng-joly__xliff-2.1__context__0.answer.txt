          <target>This is the opening chapter of the book. The author himself says that, having once passed through a season of dream and illusion, he therefore concealed the true facts, and, borrowing the tale of the “Spiritual Understanding,” composed this book, The Story of the Stone. Hence the name Zhen Shiyin (True Matters Concealed), and so forth. But what events and what persons are recorded in the book? He says again of himself: “Now, toiling and drifting in the dust of the world, with not one thing accomplished, I suddenly bethought me of all the girls of those days, and, weighing them one by one in careful review, I felt that in conduct and understanding they all stood above me. How is it that I, a man of full stature, should in truth fall short of those maids with their hairpins and skirts? Truly, shame is more than I can bear, and regret is of no avail; there is nothing to be done on such a day of utter helplessness! Whereupon I would fain set down in one volume, to tell all the world, the years when I leaned upon the grace of Heaven and the virtue of my forefathers — the days of broidered clothes and silken breeches, of sweet fare and full feeding; the debt I owed for the teaching of father and elders, the benefit of the counsel of masters and friends; and, down to this day, my guilt of no skill mastered and half a life squandered. My own guilt, indeed, I cannot escape; but in the women's chambers there have from first to last been those of worth, and by no means may I, for my own unworthiness, shield my own shortcomings and let them all be blotted out. Though today I have thatch for rafters and rushes for windows, an earthen stove and a rope bed, yet the wind and dew of dawn and dusk, the willows by the steps and the flowers in the court, in no way hinder the feelings and the brush within my breast. And though I am without learning and write without polish, what harm if I use feigned words and rustic speech to piece out a tale? It may make the women's chambers known to the world, and may also please men's eyes and drive away their gloom — is that not fitting?” Hence the name Jia Yucun (Feigned Words and Rustic Speech), and so forth.</target>

          <source>空空道人遂向石頭說道：“石兄，你這一段故事，据你自己說有些趣味 ，故編寫在此，意欲問世傳奇．据我看來，第一件，無朝代年紀可考，第二 件，并無大賢大忠理朝廷治風俗的善政，其中只不過几個异樣女子，或情或 痴，或小才微善，亦無班姑，蔡女之德能．我縱抄去，恐世人不愛看呢。” 石頭笑答道：“我師何太痴耶！若云無朝代可考，今我師竟假借漢唐等年紀 添綴，又有何難？但我想，歷來野史，皆蹈一轍，莫如我這不借此套者，反 倒新奇別致，不過只取其事体情理罷了，又何必拘拘于朝代年紀哉！再者， 市井俗人喜看理治之書者甚少，愛适趣閒文者特多．歷來野史，或訕謗君相 ，或貶人妻女，奸淫凶惡，不可胜數．更有一种風月筆墨，其淫穢污臭，屠 毒筆墨，坏人子弟，又不可胜數．至若佳人才子等書，則又千部共出一套， 且其中終不能不涉于淫濫，以致滿紙潘安，子建，西子，文君，不過作者要 寫出自己的那兩首情詩艷賦來，故假擬出男女二人名姓，又必旁出一小人其 間撥亂，亦如劇中之小丑然．且鬟婢開口即者也之乎，非文即理．故逐一看 去，悉皆自相矛盾，大不近情理之話，竟不如我半世親睹親聞的這几個女子 ，雖不敢說強似前代書中所有之人，但事跡原委，亦可以消愁破悶，也有几 首歪詩熟話，可以噴飯供酒．至若离合悲歡，興衰際遇，則又追蹤躡跡，不 敢稍加穿鑿，徒為供人之目而反失其真傳者．今之人，貧者日為衣食所累， 富者又怀不足之心，縱然一時稍閒，又有貪淫戀色，好貨尋愁之事，那里去 有工夫看那理治之書？所以我這一段故事，也不愿世人稱奇道妙，也不定要 世人喜悅檢讀，只愿他們當那醉淫飽臥之時，或避世去愁之際，把此一玩， 豈不省了些壽命筋力？就比那謀虛逐妄，卻也省了口舌是非之害，腿腳奔忙 之．再者，亦令世人換新眼目，不比那些胡牽亂扯，忽离忽遇，滿紙才人 淑女，子建文君紅娘小玉等通共熟套之舊稿．我師意為何如？”</source>
          <target>The Daoist of Emptiness thereupon said to the Stone: “Brother Stone, this tale of yours — by your own account there is something of interest in it, and so you have written it here, wishing to publish it abroad as a wonder of the world. Yet in my view, in the first place, there is no dynasty or year to give it a date; and in the second place, it contains no good government of great sages and loyal ministers setting the court and the customs of the realm to rights; within it there are only a few peculiar women, some passionate, some foolish, some of small talent or slight virtue, and none of them possessed of the virtue and ability of Lady Ban or Lady Cai. Even if I were to copy it out and take it away, I fear the world would not care to read it.” The Stone laughed and answered: “How foolish my master is! If you say there is no dynasty to date it by, what difficulty would there be for my master now to borrow the years of the Han or the Tang and tack them on? But I reflect that the wild histories of past ages all tread one and the same track; better that mine should not borrow their mould, and so be new and distinctive besides. It takes only the circumstances and the feelings as they are — why must it be bound so strictly to a dynasty and a date? Moreover, few of the common folk in the marketplace care to read books of government, while those who love lively and amusing prose are especially many. The wild histories of the past either slander rulers and ministers, or vilify other men's wives and daughters; their lewdness and wickedness are past all counting. There is besides one kind of writing of wind and moon, obscene and filthy and foul, a poisoned brush that ruins men's sons — and these too are past all counting. As for the books of beauties and gifted scholars, a thousand volumes issue from a single mould, and even in these there is in the end no keeping clear of lewdness and excess; so that the pages are filled with Pan An, Zijian, Xi Shi and Wenjun, when in truth the author only wanted to set forth those two love poems and flowery rhapsodies of his own, and so feigned a name for a young man and a maiden, and must needs bring on a petty villain to make mischief between them, just like the clown in a play. And the maids and serving-girls no sooner open their mouths than they speak in classical particles, and it is all either letters or philosophy. So, looking at them one by one, they are wholly self-contradictory and utterly at odds with reason and feeling. They cannot compare with these few women whom I have seen with my own eyes and heard with my own ears over half a lifetime; I dare not say they surpass all the people in the books of former ages, but their deeds and the whole course of them can likewise dispel sorrow and break through gloom, and there are a few clumsy verses and familiar sayings that can make one laugh over one's wine. As for partings and meetings, joy and grief, rise and fall and turns of fortune, these I have followed in their very tracks and would not dare to add the least invention, merely to please men's eyes and so lose the true record. The people of today — the poor are daily burdened by food and clothing, and the rich are never content at heart; even if for a moment they have a little leisure, there are greedy lusts and lecherous loves, the craving for goods and the seeking after amusement; where would they find the leisure to read those books of government? And so, as for this tale of mine, I do not wish the world to call it strange or marvellous, nor do I insist that the world should delight to read it; I only wish that when they are drunk with lust and lying full-fed, or when they would shun the world and put away their sorrow, they might take it up for a little amusement — would that not save them a little of their years and their strength? Compared with scheming after vanities and chasing after delusions, it would also spare them the harm of quarrels and gossip and the toil of hurrying about on foot. Moreover, it may give the world new eyes, unlike those old drafts of stale convention, with their idle tangles and their sudden partings and meetings, their pages full of men of talent and virtuous maidens, Zijian, Wenjun, Hongniang, Xiaoyu, and all the rest of the well-worn set. What does my master think of it?”</target>
