"This is the opening chapter of the book. The author himself says that, having "
"once passed through a phase of dream and illusion, he therefore concealed the "
"true facts, and, borrowing the tale of the 'Spiritual Understanding,' composed "
"this book, 'The Story of the Stone.' Hence the name 'Zhen Shiyin' (True Facts "
"Concealed), and so forth. But what events, and what persons, are recorded in "
"the book? He says again of himself: 'Now, toiling away in the dust of the "
"world with nothing accomplished, I suddenly called to mind all the women of "
"former days, and as I examined them one by one in detail, I felt that their "
"conduct and understanding were all above my own. How is it that I, a "
"full-grown man, am truly not the equal of these women? Truly my shame is more "
"than I can bear, and regret is of no avail—a day of utter helplessness! In "
"such a case, I wished to recount the past days when I relied on Heaven's grace "
"and my ancestors' virtue, the time of brocade clothes and silk trousers, the "
"days of sweet and rich fare—my betrayal of the kindness of my father and "
"elder brothers in teaching me, my failure toward the virtue of my teachers' "
"and friends' counsel—down to today, when I have not mastered a single skill "
"and have spent half my life in failure. I have compiled a volume to tell the "
"world: my guilt is indeed not to be escaped, but in the women's chambers there "
"have always been worthy persons, one after another, and I must never, because "
"of my own unworthiness, shield my own shortcomings and cause them all to be "
"blotted out together. Though today I have thatched beams and a door of wild "
"grass, an earthen stove and a rope bed, yet the wind and dew of morning and "
"evening, the willows by the steps and the flowers in the courtyard, have in no "
"way hindered my feelings and my brush. Though I am unlearned and my writing "
"lacks elegance, what harm is there in using feigned words and rustic speech to "
"unfold a tale? It may make the women's chambers known, and may also please the "
"eyes of the world and dispel men's sorrows—is that not fitting?' Hence the "
"name 'Jia Yucun' (False Words, Rustic Speech), and so forth."

"Vanitas the Taoist then addressed the stone, saying: 'Brother Stone, this "
"story of yours—by your own account it has some interest in it, and so you have "
"written it out here, wishing to bring it before the world as a remarkable "
"tale. In my view, in the first place, it bears no reign or date that can be "
"verified; in the second, it contains no great worthies and no great loyalists "
"who could set the court in order and reform the manners of the age. It is "
"nothing but a few strange women—some passionate, some foolish, some of small "
"talent and slight virtue—and there is not even the virtue or ability of a Lady "
"Ban or a Mistress Cai. Even if I copy it out, I fear the world will not care "
"to read it.' The stone laughed and answered: 'Master, how foolish you are! As "
"for its bearing no reign or date that can be verified, could you not, Master, "
"simply borrow the years of the Han or the Tang and attach them to it? What "
"difficulty would there be? But I think that the unofficial histories of the "
"past have all trodden one and the same path; none is like mine, which does not "
"borrow this old formula, and so is rather new and original. I take only the "
"facts and the reason in them, so why should I be bound by reign and date? "
"Moreover, few among the common folk of the marketplaces care to read books of "
"statecraft, while those who love amusing and entertaining idle writings are "
"especially many. The unofficial histories of the past either slander rulers "
"and ministers, or vilify men's wives and daughters—lewdness, cruelty and "
"wickedness beyond counting. There is also another kind, the writings of wind "
"and moon, whose obscenity and filth, whose poisonous strokes corrupt people's "
"sons—these too are beyond counting. And as for books of beauties and scholars, "
"a thousand volumes come from a single mould, and they cannot after all avoid "
"touching on lewdness and excess, until the whole page is filled with Pan An, "
"Cao Zhi, Xi Shi and Zhuo Wenjun—all merely so that the author may show off the "
"two love poems and flowery rhapsodies of his own; hence he invents the names "
"of a man and a woman, and must besides bring in some petty creature to stir up "
"trouble between them, just like the clown in a play. And the maids and serving "
"girls, the moment they open their mouths, are all 'thee' and 'thou' and "
"classical tags—nothing but letters or philosophy. Looked at one by one, they "
"are all self-contradictory and utterly unreasonable. They are not to be "
"compared with these few women whom I have seen and heard with my own eyes for "
"half my life. Though I dare not say they surpass all the persons in the books "
"of former ages, yet the circumstances and their causes may at least dispel "
"sorrow and break dullness; and there are also a few doggerel verses and "
"familiar sayings that can make one spit out one's food with laughter and serve "
"in place of wine. As for meetings and partings, joys and sorrows, and the "
"fortunes of rise and decline, these too I follow in their traces, and dare not "
"add the slightest invention, merely to please men's eyes and so lose the true "
"record. The people of today: the poor are worn out daily by food and clothing; "
"the rich again harbour hearts of discontent; and even if for a moment they "
"have a little leisure, there are still the matters of greed for lewdness and "
"love of beauty, of coveting goods and seeking amusement—where would they find "
"the time to read those books of statecraft? Therefore, in this story of mine, "
"I do not wish the world to call it wonderful or marvellous, nor do I insist "
"that the world shall take delight in reading it. I wish only that when they "
"are drunk with wine and sated with sleep, or when they would escape the world "
"and put away their sorrows, they may take it up and play with it—would it not "
"save them some of their life and strength? Compared with scheming after "
"vanities and chasing after falsehoods, it also saves them the harm of gossip "
"and quarrels, and the toil of running about on their legs. Moreover, it will "
"give the world new eyes; it is not like those old manuscripts which pull "
"things together at random, with partings and meetings coming and going without "
"reason, filled with geniuses and fair ladies—Cao Zhi, Zhuo Wenjun, Hongniang, "
"Xiaoyu—and all the common stock-in-trade. What does my Master think of it?'"

"'Mean rooms and empty halls—in years gone by, tablets of office lay thick "
"upon the bed; withered grass and dead willows—once they were the place of song "
"and dance. Cobwebs bind the carved beams, and green gauze is now pasted over "
"the matted window. Why speak of rouge still rich and powder still fragrant, "
"when the hair on both temples has turned to frost? Yesterday, on the mound of "
"yellow earth, white bones were seen away; tonight, beneath the red lamp, in "
"the bed, lie mandarin ducks. Gold fills the chest, silver fills the chest—in "
"the twinkling of an eye all men revile a beggar. Even as you sigh that "
"another's life is short, how know you that you yourself shall come home to "
"death? Though you train your sons well, you cannot be sure they will not one "
"day turn to brigandage. Though you choose the finest fare and richest food, "
"who would have thought you would drift into the streets of song? Because you "
"thought your gauze cap too small, you came at last to wear the cangue and the "
"bar. Yesterday you pitied the cold of a ragged coat; today you complain the "
"purple robe is too long. In noisy confusion, one man has hardly left the stage "
"before another mounts it, and you mistake a foreign land for your own home. "
"How absurd it all is—when all is said, it is only making wedding garments for "
"other men!' When the mad, lame Taoist heard this, he clapped his hands and "
"laughed: 'A fitting commentary, a fitting commentary!' Thereupon Shiyin said, "
"'Let us be off!' and, snatching the pilgrim's bundle from the Taoist's "
"shoulder, he threw it on his back, and without going home at all, went "
"floating away together with the mad Taoist. At once the whole neighbourhood "
"was in a stir, and everyone told it as a piece of news. When the lady Feng "
"heard this news, she wept till she was more dead than alive, and could only "
"consult with her father and send men to search everywhere; but where was any "
"word to be found? There was nothing to be done; she could not but depend upon "
"her parents for her daily living. Fortunately she still had two old maids to "
"serve her, and the three of them, mistress and servants, worked day and night "
"at needlework to sell, helping to meet her father's expenses. Though Feng Su "
"grumbled every day, there was nothing he could do."
