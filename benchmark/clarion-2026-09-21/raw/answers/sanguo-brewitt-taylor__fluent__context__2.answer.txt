chapter-1-001 = In the second year of Jian Ning, on the fifteenth day of the fourth month, the Emperor held court in the Wen De Hall. As he was ascending the throne, a wild gale suddenly sprang up from a corner of the hall, and a great green serpent was seen flying down from the beam and coiling upon the seat. The Emperor fell down in terror; his attendants hurriedly carried him into the palace, and all the officials fled and hid themselves. In a moment the serpent had vanished. Then suddenly came great thunder and torrential rain, together with hail, which did not cease until midnight and destroyed countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Jian Ning, Luoyang was shaken by an earthquake; the sea also overflowed, and the people along the coast were all swept into the sea by the great waves. In the first year of Guang He, a hen was changed into a cock. On the first day of the sixth month, a black vapor more than ten zhang long flew into the Wen De Hall. In the seventh month of autumn, a rainbow appeared over the Jade Hall; the cliffs of Wuyuan all crumbled and split. These manifold evil omens were not confined to a single kind.

chapter-1-002 = The Emperor issued an edict asking his ministers what was the cause of these calamities and portents. Cai Yong, a Gentleman of Consultation, submitted a memorial, holding that the falling of the rainbow and the changing of the hen were brought about by the meddling of women and eunuchs in the government; his words were blunt and direct. The Emperor sighed as he read the memorial, and then rose to change his clothes. Cao Jie watched him secretly from behind and told all to those about him. Thereupon he involved Cai Yong in guilt on other grounds and banished him back to his village. Afterwards Zhang Rang, Zhao Zhong, Feng Xu, Duan Gui, Cao Jie, Hou Lan, Jian Shuo, Cheng Kuang, Xia Yun and Guo Sheng, ten men in all, banded together in villainy and were known as the "Ten Regular Attendants." The Emperor trusted and favored Zhang Rang, calling him "Foster Father," and the government grew daily worse, until the hearts of the people turned to thoughts of disorder and bandits swarmed like bees.

He had long cherished great ambitions, and loved above all things to make friends with the heroes of the world. He stood seven chi and five cun tall, with ears that hung to his shoulders and hands that reached below his knees; his eyes could turn back upon his own ears. His face was like polished jade, and his lips like painted vermilion. He was a descendant of Liu Sheng, Prince Jing of Zhongshan, and a great-great-grandson of Emperor Jing of the Han; his surname was Liu, his given name Bei, his style Xuande. In former days Liu Zhen, son of Liu Sheng, had been made Marquis of Zhuolu Pavilion in the reign of Emperor Wu of the Han, but later, having been deprived of his rank through a fault concerning tribute gold, he left this branch of the family stranded in the district of Zhuo. Xuande's grandfather was Liu Xiong, and his father Liu Hong. Liu Hong had been recommended as filial and incorrupt and had also served as an official, but he died young. Xuande, orphaned as a child, served his mother with the utmost filial piety; the family being poor, he made his living by selling straw sandals and weaving mats. He lived in the village of Lou Sang in his native district. To the southeast of his house stood a great mulberry tree more than five zhang tall; seen from afar it was leafy and spreading, like the canopy of a carriage. A physiognomist said, "This household is sure to produce a man of noble rank."

chapter-1-007 = Zhang Fei said, "Behind my farm there is a peach garden where the blossoms are now at their fullest. Tomorrow let us go into the garden and sacrifice to Heaven and Earth, and the three of us will become sworn brothers, joining strength and heart as one, so that afterward we may compass great deeds." Xuande and Yunchang answered with one voice, "That would be most excellent." The next day, in the peach garden, they made ready a black ox, a white horse and all the other offerings of sacrifice; the three burned incense and bowed twice, and made this oath: "Mindful of Liu Bei, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei, though of different surnames, we have become brothers; therefore we shall be of one heart and one strength, to succor the distressed and uphold those in peril; above, to repay our country, and below, to bring peace to the common people. We do not ask to be born in the same year, the same month and the same day, but we ask to die in the same year, the same month and the same day. May the Sovereign Heaven and the Earth Queen truly bear witness to this our heart. If we betray righteousness and forget gratitude, may Heaven and man alike put us to death." When the oath was finished, they bowed to Xuande as the eldest brother, Guan Yu next, and Zhang Fei as the youngest. When the sacrifice to Heaven and Earth was over, they slaughtered an ox and set out wine, gathered the brave men of the countryside to the number of more than three hundred, and drank themselves into a fine carouse in the peach garden. On the morrow they set their weapons in order, lamenting only that they had no horses to ride.
