    <string name="chapter-1-001">On the fifteenth day of the fourth month of the second year of Jianning, the Emperor held court in the Wende Hall. As he ascended the throne, a wild wind suddenly rose from a corner of the hall, and there appeared a great green serpent that flew down from the beam and coiled upon the throne. The Emperor fell down in terror; his attendants hurriedly carried him into the palace, and all the officials fled in all directions. In a moment the serpent was gone. Then suddenly came great thunder and rain, together with hail, which did not cease until midnight, destroying countless houses. In the second month of the fourth year of Jianning, there was an earthquake at Luoyang; again the sea overflowed, and the people along the coast were all swept into the sea by the great waves. In the first year of Guanghe, hens turned into roosters. On the first day of the sixth month, a black vapor over ten zhang long flew into the Wende Hall. In the seventh month of autumn, a rainbow appeared at the Jade Hall; the cliffs of Mount Wuyuan all crumbled and split. These manifold ill omens were not confined to a single kind.</string>

    <string name="chapter-1-002">The Emperor issued an edict asking his ministers the cause of these calamities.     <string name="chapter-1-006">This man was not much given to reading. He was of a broad and gentle nature, sparing of words, and showed neither joy nor anger on his face. In former days, Liu Zhen, son of Liu Sheng, had been enfeoffed as Marquis of Zhuolu Pavilion in the time of Emperor Wu of Han; later, for a fault concerning tribute money, he lost his marquisate, and thus this branch of the family came to be left in the county of Zhuo. Xuande's grandfather was Liu Xiong, his father Liu Hong. Liu Hong had been recommended as Filial and Incorrupt, and had also once served as an official, but he died early. Xuande lost his father in childhood and served his mother with the utmost filial piety; the family being poor, he made a living by selling straw sandals and weaving mats.     <string name="chapter-1-012">At this time Zhang Jiao had a rebel host of one hundred and fifty thousand, while Lu Zhi had fifty thousand men; they stood facing each other at Guangzong without either gaining the upper hand.
