chapter-1-001 = This is the opening of the first chapter. The author says of himself that, having once passed through a round of dream and illusion, he concealed the true facts, and under cover of the tale of the "Spiritual Understanding" composed this book, The Story of the Stone. Hence the name Zhen Shiyin, "Truth Concealed," and so forth. But what deeds and what persons does the book record? He speaks again of himself: "Now, amid the dust and hurry of the world, having accomplished nothing, I suddenly thought back upon all the women of those days, and examined them one by one, and felt that their conduct and understanding were in every way above my own. Why then am I, a full-grown man with beard and brows, truly not the equal of those girls with their hairpins and skirts? In truth, my shame is more than I can bear, and my regret is of no avail — this is a day of utter helplessness! And so I wished to set down all the days of old when I relied upon Heaven's grace and my forefathers' virtue, the days of brocade clothes and silk trousers, of surfeit on sweet and rich fare; my betrayal of the kindness of my father and elder brothers' teaching, my failure in the virtue of my teachers' and friends' admonitions; and my present guilt of having mastered not a single skill and wasted half a lifetime — to compose it all into one volume and make it known to all the world: my sins I cannot escape, but within the women's chambers there have truly been, from first to last, women of worth, and I must not, for my own unworthiness, shield my own shortcomings and let them all be blotted out. Though today my rafters are of thatch and my windows of bramble, my stove of tile and my bed of rope; though I face wind and dew morning and evening, willows by the steps and flowers in the court — none of this has hindered my heart or my pen and ink. And though I am unlearned and my pen has no style, what harm is there in using false words and rustic speech to spin out a story, so that the women of the inner chambers may be made known, and the eyes of the world may also be pleased and men's sorrows dispelled? Is that not fitting?" Hence the name Jia Yucun, "False Words Preserved," and so forth.

chapter-1-003 = It came about that when the goddess Nüwa smelted stones to repair the heavens, she smelted at the Great Fable Mountain, on the Unfathomable Cliff, thirty-six thousand five hundred and one stones, each twelve zhang high and twenty-four zhang square. The Queen Nüwa used only thirty-six thousand five hundred of them, and there remained just one single stone unused, which she cast aside at the foot of the Green Ridge Peak on that mountain. chapter-1-004 = The Taoist of the Void then said to the Stone, "Brother Stone, this story of yours — by your own account there is something of interest in it, and so you have written it down here, hoping to make its marvels known to the world. As I see it, in the first place there is no dynasty or year-date that can be verified; and in the second place there are no great sages or great loyal ministers with good government to order the court and reform the manners of the age. In it there are only a few unusual women — some amorous, some foolish, some of small talent or slight virtue — and none of them has the virtue or ability of Lady Ban or Cai Wenji. Even if I copied it out and took it away, I fear the world would not care to read it." The Stone laughed and answered, "How foolish you are, Master! If you say no dynasty can be verified, then why should you not simply borrow the year-dates of the Han or the Tang and append them? What difficulty would there be in that? But to my mind, the unofficial histories of past ages all tread one and the same rut; better that mine should not borrow such trappings — then it will be all the newer and more distinctive. For I take only the true stuff and reason of the matter; why must one be so bound by dynasty and year-date? Besides, among the common folk of the marketplace, few care to read books on government and order, while many more love amusing and idle writing. The unofficial histories of past ages have either slandered rulers and ministers or disparaged other men's wives and daughters; their lewdness and villainy are beyond counting. And there is a further kind of writing on wind and moonlight, whose obscenity and foul stench, whose poisoned strokes, corrupt the young — and these too are beyond counting. As for the books of beauties and gifted scholars, again a thousand volumes come out of one single mold, and in the end they cannot avoid touching on lewdness, so that the page is filled with Pan An, Cao Zijian, Xi Shi and Zhuo Wenjun — all because the author wants to show off his own two love poems and flowery rhapsodies, and so invents the names of a man and a woman, and must needs bring in a petty villain to stir up trouble between them, just like the clown in a play. And their maidservants, the moment they open their mouths, speak in 'yea' and 'verily,' and talk of nothing but letters and reason. So, looking at them one by one, they are all self-contradictory, quite contrary to reason and feeling — indeed no better than these few women whom I have seen with my own eyes and heard with my own ears for half a lifetime. Though I dare not say they surpass all the persons in the books of earlier ages, still their deeds and the whys and wherefores of their lives can dispel care and break through melancholy; and there are also a few indifferent poems and familiar sayings that can make one sputter with laughter and pass the wine around. As for partings and reunions, joy and sorrow, rise and fall, and the turns of fortune, I have again followed in their tracks and trodden in their footsteps, not daring to add the least fabrication, and so merely cater to men's eyes and lose the true transmission. As for people today, the poor are daily burdened by clothing and food, while the rich are eaten up with discontent; and even if for a moment they have leisure, they have their lust for lewdness and craving for beauty, their greed for goods and their hunting after diversion — where would they find the time to read books on government and order? Therefore, as for this story of mine, I do not wish the world to call it strange or praise it as wondrous, nor do I insist that the world take delight in reading it over; I wish only that when they lie drunken and sated with lust, or when they would shun the world and escape their cares, they might take it up and amuse themselves with it — would that not save some of their life's strength? Compared with scheming after vanities and chasing after falsehoods, it would also spare them the harm of quarrelsome tongues and the hardship of bustling to and fro on their legs. Moreover, it will give the world a pair of fresh eyes, unlike those old manuscripts that drag in everything at random, where people part and meet by turns, and every page is full of men of talent and virtuous maidens — Cao Zijian, Zhuo Wenjun, Hongniang, Xiaoyu — and all the common stock-in-trade. What does my Master think of it?"

chapter-1-006 = In those days the earth sank in the southeast. In that southeastern corner there was a place called Gusu, and in it a city called Changmen, which was one of the foremost spots of wealth and fashion in the world of red dust. Outside this Changmen Gate there was a Shili Street; in the street there was a Renqing Lane; and in the lane there was an old temple, which, because the place was cramped and narrow, everyone called the Gourd Temple. Beside the temple lived a country gentleman named Zhen Fei, whose courtesy name was Shiyin. His principal wife was of the Feng family, a woman of virtuous and gentle nature, deeply versed in propriety and right. Though the household was not especially rich or noble, his family was nonetheless reckoned one of the leading houses of the district. Because this Zhen Shiyin was by nature placid and unmoved by ambition, giving no thought to office or fame, and took his daily pleasure only in admiring flowers, tending bamboos, pouring wine and chanting poems, he was in truth of the caliber of the immortals. Only one thing was lacking: he was now past fifty, and had no son at his knee; he had but one daughter, whose infant name was Yinglian, just three years old.
